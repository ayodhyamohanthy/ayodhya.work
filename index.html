02 — About

How I work.

Portrait

I'm a product designer based in Bengaluru. I got into design through engineering — which means I tend to start with the data model and the edge cases, then work outward to the screen.

Most of my work sits in the uncomfortable middle: products with too many features, used by experts, sold to people who aren't. My job is usually less about adding and more about deciding what earns a place on the screen.

Outside of that: long-distance running, bad film photography, and an unreasonable interest in transit maps.

01

Start with the constraint, not the canvas

Every strong solution I've shipped came from a hard limit — legacy data, a two-week window, one engineer.

02

Prototype ugly, decide early

Low fidelity in hour two beats high fidelity in week two. I'd rather be wrong quickly.

03

Design the system, ship the screen

Components and tokens are how a design survives after the handoff.

04

Measure the outcome

If we can't say what changed for the user, we decorated.

How a project moves

trigger → 3 steps → 1 loop

trigger

New brief

A problem arrives, usually with a constraint already attached to it.

step 01

Map the constraint

Legacy data, a two-week window, one engineer. Written down before anything is sketched.

condition

Is the constraint actually real?

Something I can negotiate — or physics?

yes
step 02a

Design inside the limit

The constraint becomes the concept. Edge cases get drawn before the happy path.

no
step 02b

Go find the real one

Interviews, instrumentation, session replays. Come back with evidence, not opinions.

outcome

Ship, then measure

If nothing changed for the user, it was decoration — and the loop starts again.
